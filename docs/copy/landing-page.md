## Section 3 — About this system

**Heading:** About this system

**Body:**

This is the official register of postgraduate students in the Department of
Education. It keeps one accurate, current record for each student, held
securely by the department.

It replaces the shared spreadsheet the department has been using. That
spreadsheet worked, but it had real problems: anyone with the link could open
it, several versions circulated at once, and it was never clear which one was
current. A single secure system fixes all three.

**Who can see your record**

Two people, and no one else:

- The **Postgraduate Coordinator**, who maintains the register.
- The **Chairperson**, through a read-only link that can be withdrawn at any
  time.

Students cannot see the register. You cannot see another student's record, and
no other student can see yours. Every time anyone opens the register, the system
records who it was and when.

**What we store**

Ten pieces of information, and nothing else:

*About you:* full name, admission or registration number, email address, phone
number.

*About your studies:* programme, degree level, year of admission.

*About your research:* research title, supervisors, current stage.

**What we do not do**

We do not collect your national ID number, your date of birth, a photograph, any
financial information, or any biometric data. We do not use your information for
advertising. We do not share it with anyone outside the department, and we never
sell it.

**What is coming later**

Future versions will add proposal uploads, supervisor access, and progress
tracking through to defence. Those are not part of this version.

## Section 4 — Why your information matters

**Heading:** Why we're asking, and why it helps you

**Card 1 — Your progress is tracked accurately**
When your stage, title, and supervisors are current, your supervisors and the
department can see where you are and support you properly.

**Card 2 — You only tell us once**
Submit your details a single time. The department uses the same record for all
internal coordination, so you are not asked for the same information again and
again.

**Card 3 — You hear about things that matter**
Your email address lets us tell you about seminars, deadlines, funding calls,
and defence schedules. Nothing else.

**Card 4 — Your rights are protected**
Under the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019 you can ask for a copy of your record,
ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. We respond within the time limits
the law sets.

**Card 5 — Fewer delays at milestones**
Accurate records mean less back-and-forth when you reach defence and graduation.

**Card 6 — A connection after you graduate**
If you choose to opt in, we will invite you to alumni events, mentorship, and
research collaborations after you finish.

We keep your record until you graduate, and then for five more years. After that

# Decisions needed before sign-off

## Note A — What we can honestly say about encryption

The original specification asked the site to tell students their data is
"encrypted in transit and at rest."

The first half is true and will stay. The second half needs care. The system is
planned for shared hosting, where the department does not control the physical
server or the database's underlying storage. We can encrypt specific sensitive
items inside the application before they are stored, and we will. We cannot
honestly describe the whole register as encrypted at rest.

The wording in Section 5, Column 1 above is what we can stand behind. It is less
impressive than the original, and it has the advantage of being true. A privacy
notice that overstates protection is itself a compliance problem, and this
particular claim could be checked by anyone who asked where the system is
hosted.

**If the department would rather make the stronger claim,** the system needs to
move to a dedicated server, which costs roughly `[EUR 5]` a month and makes the
original sentence accurate. That is a decision about budget, not about wording.

**Please choose:** keep the honest wording, or move hosting so the stronger
claim becomes true.

## Note B — Consent, or a notice?

The original specification asked students to tick a consent box before
submitting the registration form.

There is a problem with that, and it is worth a moment of the department's
attention. Kenyan data protection regulations require consent to be freely
given. A postgraduate student asked by their own department to register is not
really in a position to say no. And if consent is the legal basis, a student can
withdraw it at any time, at which point the department would be obliged to
delete a record it needs in order to administer that student's studies.

The alternative is to rely on the department's legitimate interest in keeping an
accurate register of its own students, and to replace the consent box with a
clear notice that the student acknowledges:

> I have read the Privacy Notice and I understand how the Department of
> Education will use my information.

This is not a loophole. It is the basis most universities rely on for their own
student records, and it comes with the same obligations: the department must
still tell students what it collects and why, and students keep every right they
had before, including access, correction, and deletion.

The newsletter is different. That is a genuine free choice, it is separate from
the register, and it stays as consent with a tick box and a confirmation email.

**This is a decision for the department and its Data Protection Officer, not for
the development team.** We will build whichever is chosen. If the department
prefers to keep a consent box on the registration form, tell us and we will
build it exactly as originally specified.
